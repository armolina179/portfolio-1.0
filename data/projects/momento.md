## Overview

A minimalist single-page site for designing and ordering personalized travel journals. Built on a soft, warm-neutral palette drawn from Japanese woodblock print aesthetics, it pairs a dynamic cost calculator with a contact form to deliver a keepsake storefront. The project grew out of a genuine interest in travel and memory-making into a conceptually complete product.

Key takeaway: Fulfilling technical requirements and making something worth keeping are not mutually exclusive goals.

## The Work

### Research →

Drew inspiration from the aesthetics of Japanese woodblock prints and the tradition of travel journaling as a reflective practice. Identified three core user types: travelers seeking a curated keepsake, educators wanting a customizable documentation tool, and memory collectors drawn to narrative design.

### Planning →

Sketched wireframes in Figma covering five sections: hero, journal preview, cost calculator, contact form, and footer. Selected a warm neutral palette with serif typefaces to evoke a handwritten, tactile quality. Planned light and dark mode as a core accessibility feature.

### Prototyping →

Built with vanilla HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript. The cost calculator dynamically updates totals based on journal format and add-on selections, with tax and flat-rate shipping computed in real time. The contact form uses client-side validation. Light/dark mode toggles via a CSS custom property swap.

### Testing →

Tested layout responsiveness across screen sizes, adjusted form input accessibility, and validated calculator logic against edge cases such as no selections or empty fields. Refined spacing and type scale to maintain design intent at mobile widths.

### Output →

A fully functional, self-contained single-page site that reads as a real product; clean enough to ship, personal enough to matter.

## Reflection

- Designing around a concept I genuinely care about kept every decision purposeful, which produced a more cohesive result than working from a neutral brief
- Building the cost calculator from scratch sharpened my understanding of dynamic DOM updates and real-time state without a framework
